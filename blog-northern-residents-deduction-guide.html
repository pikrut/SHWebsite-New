Northern Residents Deduction (2026): Who Qualifies and How to Claim It

Living in a place described as “northern” or “remote” does not automatically qualify you for northern residents deductions. Check your exact residence against CRA’s prescribed-zone list before claiming. This guide concerns the 2025 return filed in 2026.

Who qualifies?

You must have lived in a prescribed zone for at least six consecutive months, beginning or ending in the tax year. The period can cross December 31. Use CRA’s list to distinguish Zone A from Zone B; do not infer eligibility from the city names in our service areas.

Residency deduction

Zone A’s basic amount is $11 per eligible day; Zone B receives half. An equal additional amount may apply if you maintained and lived in a dwelling and you are the only person claiming the basic amount for that dwelling for those days. If both occupants claim the basic amount for the same days, neither can claim the additional amount for those days. Special-work-site benefits can reduce the claim.

Travel deduction

Employer travel benefits are not essential: eligible residents may instead allocate part of a $1,200 annual standard amount for each eligible individual. This is not $1,200 per trip. A person’s standard amount and employment travel-benefit method cannot both be used for that person in the same year.

For each trip, the calculation compares the applicable benefit or allocated standard amount, eligible travel costs, and lowest return airfare. Zone B claims are reduced by half. Generally, up to two personal trips per eligible individual qualify each year; eligible medical trips have no such numerical limit. Consult CRA’s travel guidance for the detailed conditions.

How to claim

Complete Form T2222 and carry the result to line 25500. Keep evidence of residence, eligible days, travel expenses and the airfare calculation. Review household allocations before filing.

Official sources

Source check: September 13, 2026. General information; eligibility depends on your circumstances.
